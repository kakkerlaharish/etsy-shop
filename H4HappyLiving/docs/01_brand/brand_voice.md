# H4HappyLiving Brand Voice

## Source and purpose

This guide translates the H4HappyLiving strategy into repeatable writing behavior. It was synchronized on 2026-07-30 with the current Canva Brand Kit, brand board, and live Organize, Learn, Create, and Thrive designs.

Canva now contains a dedicated Brand Voice guideline synchronized with this document. The Canva version is intentionally condensed for its field limits; this file remains the detailed source of truth. Incidental generated details such as invented events, dates, venues, and calls to action are not brand standards.

## Voice in one line

**A capable, caring guide who makes everyday progress feel possible.**

The brand sounds like someone who understands that real life is busy, offers a useful next step, and leaves room for the customer to choose what works for them.

## Canva Brand Voice implementation

The Canva Brand Kit uses a 384-character summary, within Canva's 500-character summary limit:

> H4HappyLiving sounds like a capable, caring guide who makes everyday progress feel possible. Use a warm, clear, practical, encouraging, and trustworthy tone across Etsy listings, product instructions, social content, campaigns, and customer care. Help people feel more capable, calm, and supported without pressure or perfection. Write for real homes, real routines, and flexible use.

Its Do and Don't fields reinforce benefit-led writing, plain US English, accurate product details, flexible language, pillar-specific tone, no pressure or fabricated claims, and no medical or guaranteed outcomes.

## Core voice traits

### Warm

Sound human, welcoming, and considerate. Recognize the customer's situation without overdramatizing it.

Use: “Bring a little more calm to your week.”

Avoid: “Your chaotic week is finally over!”

### Clear

Use familiar words, short sentences, and visible next steps. State product details precisely.

Use: “Download the PDF, choose your page size, and print at 100%.”

Avoid: “Leverage the included assets to optimize your planning workflow.”

### Practical

Connect every feature to a real use. Tell customers what a product helps them do, not merely how attractive it is.

Use: “Keep meals, groceries, and prep notes together in one clear place.”

Avoid: “A gorgeous planner you will be obsessed with.”

### Encouraging

Make progress feel accessible. Give permission to adapt, pause, restart, or use only what helps.

Use: “Start with one page and make it work your way.”

Avoid: “No excuses—complete every page for the best results.”

### Trustworthy

Be specific, accurate, and transparent. Set expectations about formats, delivery, editing, printing, limitations, and support.

Use: “This is a digital download; no physical item will be shipped.”

Avoid: “Everything you need for the perfect routine.”

## Tone controls

The voice stays consistent, while tone changes by context. Think of tone across three controls:

| Context | Warmth | Energy | Detail |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Brand statement | High | Medium | Low |
| Etsy listing | Medium-high | Medium | High |
| Product instructions | Medium | Low | High |
| Social post | High | Medium-high | Low |
| Customer support | High | Low-medium | High |
| Wellbeing content | High | Low | Medium |
| Error or problem notice | High | Low | High |
| Promotion | Medium-high | Medium | Medium |

Never raise energy by adding pressure, excessive punctuation, all caps, or unrealistic promises.

## Pillar voices

### Organize

**Emotional goal:** relief and clarity.

**Approved Canva message:** “Make room for what matters.”

**Supporting language:** simple tools, calmer days, clear priorities, one place, gentle reset, easier to see.

**Sound like:** composed, useful, and quietly motivating.

**Example:** “Put the week in one easy-to-see place, then focus on what matters most.”

Avoid implying that a tidy home or complete planner is a moral achievement.

### Learn

**Emotional goal:** curiosity and confidence.

**Approved Canva message:** “Grow a little every day.”

**Supporting language:** curious minds, practical learning, explore, discover, build confidence, learn at your pace.

**Sound like:** inviting, patient, and interested.

**Example:** “A simple activity that turns ten curious minutes into meaningful practice.”

Avoid school-like pressure, intelligence labels, guaranteed achievement, and invented events or credentials.

### Create

**Emotional goal:** expression and playful possibility.

**Approved Canva message:** “Make it personal.”

**Supporting language:** create with care, your style, make something, creative moment, thoughtful details, designed for you.

**Sound like:** open, bright, and permissive.

**Example:** “Choose the colors, add your own details, and make the finished piece feel like you.”

Avoid implying that artistic skill is required or that there is one correct result.

### Thrive

**Emotional goal:** grounded hope and self-directed progress.

**Approved Canva message:** “Progress, your way.”

**Supporting language:** gentle tools, everyday wellbeing, small step, supportive rhythm, reset, notice what helps.

**Sound like:** calm, respectful, and nonjudgmental.

**Example:** “Use the tracker in the way that supports you—there is no perfect streak.”

Avoid medical claims, diagnosis language, body judgment, compulsory positivity, and rigid wellness rules.

## Writing mechanics

### Sentence style

- Prefer short or medium-length sentences.
- Use active voice and concrete verbs.
- Address the customer as “you” when it feels natural.
- Use “we” only when speaking about genuine H4HappyLiving decisions or support.
- Use contractions naturally to keep the voice human.
- Vary sentence length enough to avoid robotic copy.
- Keep one main idea per sentence in instructions.

### Word choice

Prefer everyday verbs: plan, choose, print, notice, make, learn, begin, organize, adjust, download, share, and try.

Choose concrete benefits over abstract superlatives. “See meals and groceries together” is stronger than “transform your lifestyle.”

### Headings and calls to action

- Use sentence case.
- Make headings informative before clever.
- Keep calls to action direct and low-pressure.
- Use at most one exclamation mark in a short social caption and usually none elsewhere.

Preferred calls to action:

- “See what is included.”
- “Choose the layout that works for you.”
- “Start planning your way.”
- “Download and begin when you are ready.”
- “Save this idea for later.”

Avoid:

- “Buy now before it is gone!”
- “You NEED this!”
- “Do not miss out!!!”

## Vocabulary

### Preferred language

calm, clear, simple, thoughtful, practical, flexible, everyday, useful, gentle, curious, make room, small step, your way, ready when you are, thoughtfully designed, real life.

### Use carefully

- **Happy:** connect it to moments or experiences, not a permanent emotional state.
- **Wellbeing:** keep it general and never medicalize the product.
- **Productive:** focus on useful progress, not constant output.
- **Family:** include varied households and do not assume children or traditional roles.
- **Easy:** use only when the action is genuinely straightforward.

### Avoid

life-changing, must-have, perfect, fail-proof, guaranteed, miracle, hack, boss mom, lazy, no excuses, hurry, obsessed, cure, treat, anxiety-free, stress-free, transform overnight.

## Channel guidance

### Etsy titles

Write for clarity and search relevance. Name the product and its strongest differentiators. Do not fill the title with repeated keywords or emotional claims.

### Etsy listing opening

Use this sequence:

1. Customer benefit.
2. Real-life use case.
3. Plain explanation of the product.

Example: “Bring a little more calm to your week. This flexible family meal planner keeps meals, groceries, and prep notes together in one clear place.”

### Etsy details

Use scannable headings and bullets. State what is included, file formats, sizes, editing options, printing guidance, delivery method, and what is not included. Never hide essential limitations beneath promotional copy.

### Product instructions

Lead with the shortest successful path. Use numbered steps, one action at a time, and visible troubleshooting. Avoid promotional language inside instructions.

Example: “Open the PDF. Choose the page you want. Print at actual size or 100%.”

### Social media

Use one useful or relatable idea per post. Keep the main visual message short. Captions can add a practical tip, gentle prompt, or specific product connection.

Example: “A five-minute reset can make tomorrow feel lighter. Write down the three things that matter most, then let the rest wait.”

Review AI-generated Canva designs carefully. Delete invented event information, filler invitations, generic venue details, fake testimonials, and claims that are unrelated to the product.

### Customer support

Use the sequence: acknowledge, clarify, solve, confirm.

Example: “I am sorry the file gave you trouble. Please tell me which device and PDF reader you are using, and I will help you get it working.”

Do not blame the customer, quote policy before understanding the problem, or over-apologize without offering a next step.

### Promotions

State the genuine offer, eligible items, end date, and relevant conditions. Keep urgency factual. Never fabricate scarcity or use guilt.

### Wellbeing content

Use supportive, optional phrasing such as “you might try,” “if it helps,” and “use what works for you.” Add appropriate disclaimers when a customer could mistake the product for professional advice.

## Message patterns

### Benefit-led formula

**Benefit + product + practical mechanism**

“Bring more clarity to busy mornings with a visual routine chart that keeps each step easy to see.”

### Gentle invitation formula

**Small possibility + low-pressure action**

“A calmer week can begin with one clear page. Choose your layout when you are ready.”

### Feature-to-benefit formula

**Feature + so that + customer outcome**

“Includes US Letter and A4 files so you can print with the paper size you already use.”

### Support formula

**Acknowledge + next step + reassurance**

“I can see why that was confusing. Try printing at 100%, and if the size is still off, send me a screenshot and I will help.”

## Grammar and formatting

- Use US English by default unless a product targets another market.
- Use the serial comma for clarity.
- Spell out one through nine in prose; use numerals for measurements, steps, prices, ages, and product specifications.
- Write dates unambiguously: July 30, 2026.
- Use `%` with numerals.
- Use en dashes only for ranges; use em dashes sparingly.
- Avoid all caps except very short functional labels where accessibility remains strong.
- Keep emoji optional and minimal; never use emoji in place of essential meaning.

## Review checklist

Before publishing, confirm:

- The customer benefit comes first.
- The official name is written as H4HappyLiving.
- The tone matches the channel and pillar.
- Copy is specific about the product, format, and next step.
- Sentences are clear, active, and easy to scan.
- The customer retains choice and is not judged or pressured.
- Claims are accurate and appropriately limited.
- Wellbeing language is non-medical and non-diagnostic.
- Generated filler, fake details, and irrelevant template content are removed.
- The copy sounds human when read aloud.
